# ADR-0024 — A proof module's lemma graph is read from the parser, a reading of a proof is a row pinned to the proof's text, and a tool reports both and never supplies a reading

**Status:** Proposed · 2026.11.22
**Chosen by:** the custodian (that the function is tla-kit's, with the rest of what explains a specification, and
that it is drafted on `main` as a record of its own). The assistant chose the rest: the parser as the source, what
is listed of each fact, the readings table and its pin, the arrows check, and the name. Seven choices are listed as
owed, below.
**State:** Built ahead of acceptance, as working output: `server/lemma_graph.py`, the server's `lemma_graph` tool,
`tla-kit lemma-graph`, and `server/test_lemma_graph.py`. In no release. This repository has no readings table,
because it has no proof module of its own.
**Depends on:** ADR-0006 (what a tool may write), ADR-0008 items 1 and 3 (the standard library only; every path
confined to the project), ADR-0007 (the skill's library, which finds the checker and a module's chain).
**Relates to:** ADR-0009 (`prove`, which says whether a proof is accepted; this does not). ADR-0023 (Proposed, on
another branch): why a claim stops short of a proof, where this says how a proof goes when there is one.
`GRAPH-AND-DIAGRAMS.md` §2.1, whose graph holds a proof module's relation to its module as one *proved* edge: this
is what is inside that edge.
**Pending:** none.

---

## Context

tla-kit can say a good deal about a proof module. `prove` says which obligations fail and why. `proof_results`
writes the results file, `proof_sync` says whether it is still the file of this tree, and `symbols` lists the
theorems' names. Nothing says how the proof is put together: which lemma rests on which, what is cited from a
library, which definitions a step unfolds, and what a person understood of it.

A reader gets that today in one of three ways, and each has failed once.

- **By reading the proof.** TOPAK's `Workflow_proof.tla` is 1,312 lines: 44 lemmas and theorems, 74 citations
  between them.
- **From a graph drawn by hand.** One was drawn of that module on 2026.11.21, by the assistant, in a draft
  "blueprint" of the proof. Compared with the citations the proofs make, it left out fourteen and drew three
  hypotheses as citations (TOPAK pull request #102). Its names, lines and digests had all been checked by hand.
- **From a reader that works on patterns over the text.** The generator named in the provenance note does this for
  one module, and checks the arrows it draws against what it read. Unchanged, on `Workflow_proof.tla` it merged
  facts and found 22 of the 33 lemmas: it expects a blank line between facts, where TOPAK's proofs put a rule, and
  numbered step labels, where they also write `<1>a`. The layout of a file is not its syntax.

The words that explain a proof have the same trouble as the picture. They are written by a person or an assistant
beside the proof, nothing ties them to it, and an assistant asked how a proof goes will say something plausible
whether or not anyone has read it.

## Decision

1. **A fact is a named assumption, lemma, proposition, corollary or theorem of a module.** Its kind is the keyword
   it was written with. An unnamed theorem is listed and cannot be cited.
2. **A citation is read from the parser, not from the text's layout.** The script takes SANY's XML export of a
   copy of the module, as `symbols` does, and reads each proof's facts as the references SANY resolved. An arrow
   from A to B says that B's proof names A after `BY`, or in a `USE` step. A step's label is never a citation of a
   fact. What a `HIDE` step names is not one.
3. **For each fact it lists** what its proof cites in the module; what it cites elsewhere, told apart as another
   module of the project or a library; the prover's tactics it names (definitions of the module `TLAPS`, such as
   `PTL`), as backends and not as facts; the definitions after `DEF`; the project's definitions its statement
   names; what cites it; every fact it rests on, and how deep that goes; and its steps and its leaves by kind.
4. **It reads the text and does not run the prover.** It never says a proof was accepted: that is `prove`'s answer
   and a results file's, and the report's header says so. What it can say from the text is the reverse: a fact
   with a leaf that is `OMITTED`, or stated with no proof, is listed as not proved in the text, with every fact
   that rests on it.
5. **What it cannot place, it counts and does not interpret:** a definition named as a fact, a fact that is an
   expression, a `BY ONLY`. A `USE` or `HIDE` at the level of the module is listed with its line, and is not
   counted as a citation of any one fact below it.
6. **A reading is one line, written by a person, of how a proof goes or what a definition is for.** Readings are
   rows of a table, by default `spec/readings.tsv`: tab-separated, `#` lines for comments, a header row. Its
   columns are `module` (by its path from the project root), `fact`, `pin`, `reading`, `by` and `date`.
7. **A reading is pinned to the text it was a reading of.** The pin is the first 16 hex digits of the sha256 of the
   fact's own lines, without trailing blanks, and the script prints it beside each fact. A row whose pin is no
   longer the fact's is `STALE`. Reading the changed proof again and moving the pin is the review. The script
   never writes the table.
8. **A lemma or theorem with no row is `UNREAD`, and nothing fills it in.** The script does not write, suggest or
   derive a reading. A definition or an assumption may carry a reading, and one without is listed as that and is
   not counted as unread, since there is no proof of it to read.
9. **A row that cannot stand is `BAD`:** it names no fact or definition of its module, it is a second reading of
   one fact, its module is not in the project, or a cell is empty or malformed. A row for another module of the
   project is left to that module's run.
10. **`by` says who gave the reading.** A reading the assistant gave is the assistant's until the custodian takes
    it, as with a record's **Chosen by** line.
11. **A drawn graph is checked against the citations.** Given the arrows someone drew, as pairs of names, the
    script reports each arrow that is not a citation, each citation with no arrow, and each name the module does
    not have. A diagram's generator calls this before it draws.
12. **The check is `server/lemma_graph.py`,** Python 3 with the standard library only. The server gains a tool,
    `lemma_graph`, that returns its report as working output (ADR-0006 item 2), and `tla-kit lemma-graph` runs it
    in the image. It answers for a module, or for one fact with the facts it rests on.
13. **It reads the project as data and writes nothing into it.** SANY runs on a copy, in a temporary directory.
    A module or a table outside the project is refused. It exits 1 when a reading is `BAD` or `STALE` or when a
    drawn graph and the citations differ, and with `--strict` when a lemma or theorem is `UNREAD`; 2 when the
    module does not parse or a table cannot be read; otherwise 0.
14. **`READ` is not "right".** It says the text has not changed since the reading was given. Whether the reading
    is a true account of the proof is read by a person, and the report's header says so.
15. **No TLA+ module is written for it.** ADR-0015 and ADR-0016 specify a process before it is built, because
    parties act in an order. This is a function of files at one commit. This is the assistant's reading, and is
    owed (L5).

## Consequences

- **The first runs, on 2026.11.22,** natively, with the pinned jar and the library of the prover copy this
  repository keeps (`tlapm-9a52faf`):

  | Module | Facts | Definitions | Citations | Cited by none | Steps | Leaves |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | The skill's fixture, `Counter_proof.tla` | 6: 2 theorems, 4 lemmas | 0 | 4 | 2 | 5 | 9 `BY` |
  | TOPAK's `Seats_proof.tla` | 13: 3 theorems, 10 lemmas | 5 | 12 | 3 | 40 | 42 `BY`, 1 `OBVIOUS` |
  | TOPAK's `Workflow_proof.tla` | 44: 11 theorems, 33 lemmas | 21 | 74 | 10 | 457 | 378 `BY`, 10 `OBVIOUS` |

  TOPAK was read at its tree of `ffd02f1` for `spec/`. In all three every proof is written out: no leaf is
  `OMITTED` and none is missing. `Workflow_proof` names `PTL` in eleven facts and two theorems of
  `FiniteSetTheorems`; `Seats_proof` names `PTL` in three. Every lemma and theorem is `UNREAD`, since no readings
  table exists anywhere yet.
- **Then every proof module of TOPAK, thirty-five of them, in 28 seconds.** Each was read without an error: 844
  facts (589 lemmas, 221 theorems, 34 assumptions), 274 definitions the proof modules add, 1,103 citations, 5,603
  steps, and 5,105 leaves, of which 4,995 are `BY` and 110 `OBVIOUS`. No leaf is `OMITTED` and none is missing.
  `PTL` is named by 209 facts, `ExpandENABLED` by 21 and `Isa` by 10; the libraries cited are `FiniteSetTheorems`
  and `SequenceTheorems`; five modules cite facts of another module of the project. 216 lemmas and theorems are
  cited by no fact of their own module, which is each module's results and its mutation lemmas together.
- **That run found a fault in the script, and it is fixed.** `PlaneClock_proof.tla` was reported as having a
  lemma "stated with no proof". The lemma is proved; one of its steps is a `WITNESS`, which takes no proof. `TAKE`,
  `WITNESS` and `HAVE` steps are now told apart, and the fixture has one of each. A reader of this report should
  take from that what the script's header says: it is a reading of the text, and a reading can be wrong.
- **The citations agree with the two earlier counts.** For both TOPAK modules the parser gives the citations that
  a corrected pattern reader gave on 2026.11.22, twelve and seventy-four, pair for pair. The fourteen arrows the
  hand-drawn graph left out are among them.
- **This repository has nothing of its own to read.** `spec/PinLifecycle.tla` and `spec/ProveRequests.tla` have no
  proof modules. ADR-0023's first run reports the same absence from the other side.
- The server has fifteen tools on `main`. `examples/agent-repair/check.py` names the fourteen of the published
  image, and gains this one when the plugin's digest moves to a release with it.
- The image gains one file, `server/lemma_graph.py`, and no package.
- **Not built, and made possible:** a diagram of a proof module, lemma by lemma, as a third diagram beside the two
  of `GRAPH-AND-DIAGRAMS.md` §3.4, laid out from each fact's depth and checked by item 11; and a reading attached
  to the specification graph's *proved* edge, so that a stale reading is reported with the edge.

**What it does not do.**

- **It does not say a proof is right, or checked.** A graph of citations is the shape of an argument, as written.
- **It does not check a reading against its proof.** No tool does. A pin says when to read again.
- **It does not see a module-level `USE` as a citation,** though every proof after one may rest on it (item 5).
- **It has not met every construct.** It was run on thirty-seven modules. None of TOPAK's names an expression
  as a fact, which the fixture does. No module it has read cites a theorem through an `INSTANCE` or has a `USE`
  at the level of the module.
- **It reads one module at a call.** A fact cited from another module of the project is named with its module and
  is not followed there.
- **It does not explain a statement.** Whether a theorem says what its record says is the first link of the chain,
  and is read by a person.

## Alternatives not taken

- **Patterns over the text.** Tried on 2026.11.22, on TOPAK's two modules: right for one, and wrong for the other
  until two patterns were changed. The parser is in the image, and the server already calls it.
- **Add the citations to `symbols`.** Cheaper by one tool. But the readings and the arrows are checks that can
  fail, and `symbols` has nothing that can (L2).
- **A quote of the source, as ADR-0023 asks of a reason.** A reason's source is a record that says it in words. A
  reading's source is the proof, which says it in steps, so there are no words to quote, and a pin over the
  proof's text does the same work: the reading cannot outlive what it was a reading of.
- **Readings as comments in the proof module.** A proof module that a record cites by digest is frozen, as
  TOPAK's are, and a comment would move the digest.
- **Supply a reading where none is recorded.** An assistant can always produce one. It would be a guess presented
  as an account, which is what item 8 exists to stop.
- **Fail on an unread fact.** The outside generator does, for one module of seventeen facts. For forty-four it
  would be a wall, and a wall is passed by writing something (L3).
- **Run the prover, and say "proved".** A second way to make a verdict. ADR-0006 keeps verdicts to `prove` and to
  the generators.
